SHOW THE PRODUCT. Name the competitor.

Effy AI sells performance-review software into a category full of vague promises and gated demos. The homepage does the opposite: a real product screenshot in the hero, a direct comparison table naming Lattice and spreadsheets by name, and a starting price stated in the open — built to convince a busy HR buyer without ever asking them to fill in a form first.

Client
Effy AI
Category
Website design
Scope
Full homepage design
Focus
Proof over promises

Effy AI builds AI-assisted performance reviews and 360° feedback for small and mid-sized companies — a category dominated by enterprise tools like Lattice, and by nothing at all (spreadsheets). The brief was a homepage that could win a buyer's trust in one visit, using specifics instead of the category's usual vagueness. Below is that homepage, section by section, with the thinking behind each one.

01 · The hero
Effy AI

The product, not a stock photo.

The hero image is the actual dashboard — a real review, a real radar chart — not an illustration of what the product might look like. For a tool this specific, showing the interface does more convincing than another headline could.

02 · The trust signal
Effy AI

Video, not text quotes.

Three testimonials, each with a real face on a real video thumbnail instead of a name next to a headshot icon — paired with a plain company count, country count, and star rating instead of a wall of logos.

03 · The pitch
Effy AI

The pitch is what it isn't.

"Not 15 modules your employees never open" does more work than any feature list — naming the competitor's actual weakness before listing its own two pillars: one system for every review type, and goals wired directly into them.

04 · The demo
Effy AI

A demo, offered without a form.

One click to a walkthrough — no gated video, no "enter your email to watch" — for the visitor who wants to see the product before they'll talk to anyone about it.

05 · The numbers
Effy AI

Speed, price, and time saved — nothing else.

The three numbers a busy HR buyer actually stops for: no sales call to launch, a starting price stated in the open, and hours saved per review cycle instead of a vague productivity claim.

06 · The comparison
Effy AI

Naming the competitor is the pitch.

A direct table against Lattice and spreadsheets — setup time, free plan, starting price, onboarding — the exact questions a switching buyer is already asking, answered without hiding behind "request a demo."

07 · The trust layer
Effy AI

Security, in three plain sentences.

No compliance-badge wall — just three specific claims (AWS-hosted, GDPR compliant, configurable retention) written for a buyer evaluating the product, not a security auditor filing a checklist.

08 · The close
Effy AI

Three words instead of a paragraph.

The lowest-friction close on the page — no new argument, just the two buttons a convinced visitor needs: sign up, or go check pricing.

09 · The footer
Effy AI

A footer built for the second visit.

Product, case studies, and comparison pages sit alongside an "ask about Effy AI" prompt for Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Grok — a footer written for how people actually research software now, not just a sitemap.
