THE LOGO DOES three jobs at once.

LocalityIQ is a proptech startup built on two things: helping people find a home, and answering every question that comes with one. The mark carries both jobs in a single shape — a door for the home search, a question mark for the answers, and a chat-tail for how those answers actually arrive.

Client
LocalityIQ
Category
Brand design
Scope
Logo & visual identity
Focus
One mark, three meanings

LocalityIQ helps people find a home and get every question about it answered, in one place. The brief was a mark that could say both without needing a tagline to explain it. Below is that identity, from the wordmark's logic to how it carries into the product's own imagery.

01 · The wordmark

The "O" is a coordinate, not a letter.

LOCALITY's O is drawn as a plain ring — the same shape a map uses for a pin, or a degree sign uses for a coordinate. A company that sells locations gets to have one built into its own name.

02 · The mark

A door, a question mark, and a chat bubble — at once.

The "iQ" resolves into an open door (a home), while its overall silhouette reads as a question mark and its trailing corner as a chat bubble's tail — the two halves of the product, find and ask, fused into a single shape instead of two separate icons.

03 · In the world

Built to survive outside a logo file.

A wordmark this simple has to hold up applied to something physical, not just centred on a slide — here, printed large on a cushion, still legible at a glance from across a room.

04 · The icon system

The reassurance, broken into four claims.

Trust, range, financing, guidance — each one gets its own small illustrated icon instead of competing for space in one paragraph, so a nervous first-time buyer can scan the reasons to stay in under five seconds.

05 · Who it's for

A family, not a floor plan.

The brand's photography leans on the people who'll live in the home, not renders of the home itself — because the decision LocalityIQ is actually selling into is a family one.

06 · The feeling, not the flat

The roof gesture says more than a spec sheet.

Hands forming a roof over the family is a more honest pitch for what "finding a home" means to a buyer than another photo of a building's facade would be.
