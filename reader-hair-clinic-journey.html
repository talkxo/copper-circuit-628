01 — The problem

The sale ends. The story doesn't.

A hair transplant doesn't show its result on the day of surgery — new growth takes six to twelve months to come through. Most of the marketing effort, understandably, was aimed at the part before that: get the enquiry to a consult, get the consult to a booking.

Which meant the riskiest part of the relationship — the long, invisible waiting period — was also the quietest one. Leads who didn't book after a consult drifted with no further contact. Patients who did have surgery got a single post-op note, then nothing, for months, right when doubt (or a disappointing early result) was most likely to surface.

Reviews, referrals, revision consults — all left to whichever patient happened to remember to call.

02 — The insight

Nine stages, not one funnel.

The fix started with mapping the entire patient journey as it actually exists in the CRM — not "lead to sale," but every real stage a person could sit in.

ContactedAppointment scheduledConsultedThe key nurturing segmentBooked treatmentDeposit paidCompleted sessionPost-opLost / indecisive

Each stage carried its own real question, and its own natural moment to ask it. A lead who hasn't booked a consult in seven days needs a nudge, not a hard sell. A patient three weeks from surgery needs practical prep information, not marketing. A patient six months post-op is at exactly the point where new growth is becoming visible — and exactly the point most clinics have already stopped talking to them.

The insight: timing the message to the moment in the journey mattered more than the message itself.

03 — The solution

Six queues, timed to the journey.

Six automated queues were built in the CRM, each triggered by where a person actually was — not a single generic drip, but a different logic for every stage.
