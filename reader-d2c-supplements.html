01 — The problem

A category built on no loyalty at all.

Supplements buyers don't stay for a brand — they stay for whoever referred them. The category was already crowded with recognisable names, and this brand needed to win first-time trust on a fraction of their media budgets.

Customer research confirmed it early: no brand loyalty, a heavy reliance on referrals, and limited appeal to any one platform. The one real hook available was blunt and simple — great quality, the right price, and proven results — but nothing in the acquisition setup was built to prove any of the three before asking for the sale.

And every landing page had the same blind spot: a visitor who wasn't ready to buy today simply left, and left with nothing. No follow, no follow-up, no reason to come back when they were ready.

02 — The insight

Know the cohort, before you spend on it.

Instead of one generic "fitness enthusiast" persona, the research mapped real behavioural cohorts — how social they are, whether they chase quality or price, how well the product actually fits their routine, and whether they discover brands themselves or need a referral to trust one. Media, creative, and offer all followed from where a cohort actually sat on those lines, not from a guess.

03 — The solution

Two pillars: be trusted, then be found.

Perception Building did the trust work — content filtered through one question, "will a fitness enthusiast relate to it?", every product linked and tagged so interest never hit a dead end, and visibility aimed at people already consuming fitness content, not a cold audience. Powerplay Campaigns did the acquisition work — paid media strategically focused on the channels and offers most likely to convert each cohort.

BTE landing page

"Before The Explanation" — leads with the outcome, for cohorts who decide fast.

Flagship SKU page

One hero product, fully explained — for cohorts who research before buying.

Social proof page

Reviews and results up front — for the referral-driven, trust-first cohort.

Instant sale / offer page

Price-led, urgency-led — for the deal-driven cohort that needs a reason today.

Why Google Search pulled ahead of everything else

Every channel was bidding into the same flood of category search terms — multivitamins, biotin, vitamin D3, a hundred variations of the same handful of concerns. Most of it was noise: generic terms, price-shoppers, searches that cost more than they ever converted. Google Search's edge wasn't more budget — it was walking away from that noise and leaning hard into the small set of terms that actually paid back. That discipline is most of the reason the channel alone returned 5.5X.

The exit that wasn't a bounce

Every one of those four pages fed the same funnel — visitor to subscriber to engaged to buyer to advocate — but the real fix sat at the point most funnels ignore: the non-buyer. Instead of a dead end, the exit became an engagement contest, asking for a follow instead of a card number. Media mix stayed simple and accountable — 30% YouTube, 30% Google Search, 30% Facebook, 10% remarketing, reweighted as results came in.

04 — The results

5.5X back, and an audience that stayed.

The Google Search campaign alone returned 5.5X on media spend. And because the landing page gave non-buyers somewhere real to go, the brand closed with something a pure sales funnel never would have: over 2,000 new followers, and a cohort system built to keep getting sharper.
