FIGHT PROCRASTINATION, bring a crowd.

Flow Club sells virtual coworking sessions — real hosts, real members, a timer, and a webcam grid, built around the idea that willpower is overrated and other people's energy isn't. The homepage skips the abstract "focus room" pitch and leads with the actual scheduling product, real member testimonials, and a five-step session structure, instead of asking a visitor to imagine what the experience feels like.

Client
Flow Club
Category
Website design
Scope
Full homepage design
Focus
Community as the product

Flow Club is virtual coworking — book a session, show up on camera with strangers who also have work to do, and use their focus as a draft to get your own done. The brief was a homepage that sold the community and the structure, not just the concept. Below is that homepage, section by section, with the thinking behind each one.

01 · The hero
Flow Club

The product, embedded in the pitch.

The hero mockup isn't an illustration of "a scheduling app" — it's the real Upcoming Sessions screen, real session cards and all. A New York Times line does the credibility work in one sentence, so the headline gets to stay about fun, not features.

02 · Our Members
Flow Club

Named members, not stock avatars.

A university professor, a graduate student, a financial analyst, a poet — real photos, real job titles, real quotes about ADHD and body-doubling. The 1,200+ hosts and 94% stat sit above them as the numeric case, before the human one.

03 · How Flow Club Works
Flow Club

Five steps, told like a session, not a spec.

Book a session, show up, share your goals, start doing, debrief & celebrate — each one gets its own small product shot instead of a generic icon, so the sequence reads like what actually happens on a call rather than a feature list.

04 · The Power of Intentional Action
Flow Club

Three numbers, set big enough to not need a chart.

98%, 91%, 97% — professional improvement, meeting personal obligations, starting difficult tasks. No bar graph, no icon set. At that size, the numbers are the entire argument.

05 · Proof, right after the claim
Flow Club

"Professional and without judgement" gets a screenshot.

The pull quote breaks the page, then two real in-product views back it up immediately — the host directory for "our members," and a live muted-work session grid with visible goals for "our structure." The claim and its evidence sit in the same section.

06 · The press mentions
Flow Club

Credibility, sized down on purpose.

The New York Times, CNN, WSJ, and four more sit in a quiet grey strip after the member proof, not before it — a supporting signal once the case is already made, not the headline act.

07 · The method, named
Flow Club

"Body doubling" and "dopaminergic design," not "features."

Naming the actual psychological mechanisms — with a link out to explain body doubling for anyone who's never heard the term — turns what could be vague self-help language into something that reads like it was designed on purpose.

08 · The real app, live
Flow Club — Upcoming Sessions

The screenshot in the hero, and the real thing.

Full nav, real filters — Times & Durations, Themes, Hosts — a live "204 flowing in" counter, and session cards grouped by date. It's the same product shown in the marketing mockup, which is the point: nothing in the pitch is staged.
