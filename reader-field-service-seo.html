FOUND BY TRADE, not by category.

A field service management platform was fighting one brutally crowded keyword: "field service management software." The real opportunity was 43 separate trades, each searching for help in their own words — so the strategy stopped competing for one term and started owning dozens.

01 — The problem

One term. Everyone fighting for it.

The platform's core page was built to rank for "field service management software" — the obvious category term, and the most contested one in the space.

It's a page ServiceNow, Salesforce, Zoho, and every other enterprise platform is also fighting for, with content budgets a growing SaaS company can't match. Worse, the term itself is a modest pool — a few hundred searches a month, shared across every competitor category-wide. The platform was spending real effort to rank for a term that, even at position one, wouldn't move much traffic.

Meanwhile, the actual buyers — HVAC contractors, RO repair technicians, lift and escalator maintenance firms, pest control operators — weren't searching "field service management software" at all. They were searching in their own trade's language, in pools many times larger.

02 — The insight

Forty-three markets, not one.

The addressable market was never a single audience searching a single term — it was dozens of distinct trades, each with its own vocabulary, its own competitors, and search volume the generic category term couldn't touch. A plumber doesn't type "field service management." They type "plumbing service software," or don't search for software at all — they search for "ac repair" or "ac service," the job itself, and expect software to be part of what shows up.

Ranking generically would always lose to category giants with bigger budgets. Ranking as the specific, obvious answer for one trade at a time was winnable — and every trade the platform actually served was a keyword pool the enterprise players weren't bothering to fight for.

03 — The solution

A page for every trade, a funnel behind each one.

Every serviceable trade — 43 in total, from HVAC to pest control to lift maintenance — was mapped against 60 product-capability angles: real-time tracking, job scheduling, AMC management, invoicing, attendance, and more. That matrix pointed to where real search volume and real product fit actually overlapped, instead of guessing.

Instead of one core page fighting for one generic term, the team built a dedicated landing page for every trade — written in that trade's own language, targeting that trade's own keyword cluster, and funnelling into the same product from a dozen different front doors instead of one crowded one.
