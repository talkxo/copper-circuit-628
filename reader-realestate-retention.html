Gurgaon's leading real estate developer had a project running behind schedule, and buyers with nowhere to say so — except social media and public forums. 01 — The problem

There was no dialogue. At all.

The project was running behind schedule — not unusual in residential construction, but genuinely frustrating for buyers waiting to move in. What made it worse wasn't the delay itself. It was the silence around it.

There was no direct communication and no regular updates to customers. No dialogue at all. Left with nothing official to hold onto, buyers did the only thing left: they took their frustration to social media and public forums — the two places a developer's reputation is hardest to defend and easiest to damage.

02 — The insight

Grievance goes public when it has nowhere else to go.

The delay wasn't really the problem — projects run late. The problem was that a real, valid complaint had no legitimate place to land, so it went to the one place guaranteed to be seen: public. Spin about the schedule wasn't going to fix that. A reason to open the next email, and somewhere to actually be heard 1-1, would.

That meant understanding sentiment properly first — using CRM data to identify the "tribe leaders," the most vocal and most connected buyers, and their specific top issues — instead of guessing at a generic update everyone would ignore the same way.

Address the grievance

Meet the concern about the delay directly, with creative and positive content — not silence, not spin.

Understand sentiment

Use CRM data to understand individual sentiment, and identify the tribe leaders and their top issues first.

Engage and personalise

Create top-of-mind engagement, personalise the experience with the brand, and turn sentiment positive.

Create a 1-1 channel

Give every buyer a direct line, so public grievance becomes negligible by design, not by luck.
