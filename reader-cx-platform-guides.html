01 — The problem

The product page can't rank for the question.

E-commerce sellers and CX leaders don't start their search with "AI customer service platform." They start with the actual decision in front of them.

How do you restructure a support team around an AI agent? What do you even call the new roles? How do you prove to leadership that the thing is working, when the industry-standard metric — containment rate — can't tell a satisfied customer from one who just gave up? How do you get through Black Friday without every channel melting down at once?

A feature page answers none of that. It wasn't going to rank for it, and it wasn't going to earn the trust of someone trying to solve a real operational problem.

02 — The insight

Write it like a playbook, not a pitch.

The people evaluating an AI CX platform are also the people trying to solve a genuine operational problem — restructuring a team, proving ROI to leadership, surviving a seasonal traffic spike. Content that reads like an internal practitioner playbook — real job descriptions, real org charts, a formula for measuring performance instead of a vague claim — earns the kind of trust and search visibility a feature page never could.

And a resource specific to a moment — Black Friday prep, not "customer service tips" in general — captures intent no generic content calendar would ever catch, because it's ranking for exactly what someone is searching for that week.

03 — The solution

Two guides, built like internal playbooks.

Two flagship gated guides were built, each substantial enough to be genuinely useful on its own — the gate is a fair trade, not a paywall on thin content.
