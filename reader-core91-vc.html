SAY IT ONCE, say it sharp.

CORE91.VC needed a homepage that could do three jobs without diluting any of them: state who they are in one line, prove it with a portfolio most funds would be proud of, and make the ask impossible to miss. Every section earns its place — nothing is there to fill space.

Client
CORE91.VC
Category
Website design
Scope
Full homepage design
Focus
Clarity over decoration

CORE91.VC backs pre-seed to Series A founders, globally. The brief was to compress a fund's entire pitch to a founder — thesis, speed, network, proof — into one homepage, without it ever feeling like a deck. Below is that homepage, section by section, with the thinking behind each one.

01 · Opening screen
core91.vc

The opener says nothing extra.

No tagline stacked on a tagline — just the name, stated plainly on black, with a small scroll indicator underneath it. The first screen is a held breath before the thesis; everything a visitor needs arrives further down, not crammed into this one.

02 · The thesis
core91.vc

The pitch, in four lines.

One organic curve breaks the grid and holds the entire thesis — stage, geography, sector focus — in four short lines a founder can read in five seconds. The oversized "91" isn't decoration; it's the fund's own name, treated like a wordmark rather than a number.

03 · The fine print, made scannable
core91.vc

Four boxes, four reasons to reply.

Check size, decision speed, network access, and reach — the questions every founder actually has before a first call — each answered in one line, colour-coded so they read as a set instead of four unrelated bullet points.

04 · Portfolio proof
core91.vc

Proof, not adjectives.

No "trusted by industry leaders" copy — just the logos, dense and unranked, so the portfolio makes the argument for credibility itself: a wall of names a founder recognises before reading a single sentence about the fund.

05 · The track record before the fund
core91.vc

A second wall, kept separate on purpose.

The partners' personal and prior bets — Coinbase, Airmeet, AngelList among them — get their own row, visually distinct from the fund's own portfolio, so neither claim dilutes the other.

06 · The team
core91.vc

Faces, not job titles.

Four partners, four photos, four short bios that read like introductions rather than résumés — because a founder isn't underwriting a firm, they're choosing the person on the other end of the first message.

07 · The close
core91.vc

One button, one job.

The page closes on a single yellow button — "Submit your pitch" — the only saturated colour on an otherwise black-and-white site, so there's no ambiguity about what to do next.
