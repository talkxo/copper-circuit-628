PROVE IT, before you pitch it.

City Apartments needed a homepage that could carry a premium price in a category built on trust — real buildings, real scale, a real person to talk to. Every section stacks one more piece of proof before the site ever asks for the enquiry.

Client
City Apartments
Category
Website design
Scope
Full homepage design
Focus
Credibility, stacked in order

City Apartments runs premium serviced apartments for business travellers in the City of London. The brief was to make a long, expensive stay feel like an easy decision — for a corporate travel buyer who's never met the team booking it. Below is that homepage, section by section, with the thinking behind each one.

01 · The hero
City Apartments

The claim, backed before it's made.

Two proof points — 35 years of trusted service, 10,000+ happy customers — sit directly above the headline, not buried in an About page. By the time a visitor reads "we're your home," the credibility is already established.

02 · The buildings
City Apartments

The portfolio, not a promise.

Four real buildings, four real addresses, right under the fold — because "premium serviced apartments" is an easier sell once you can see the actual front doors.

03 · The reframe
City Apartments

One sentence answers the whole brief.

A full-width pause between the proof and the pitch — one line that reframes "serviced apartments" as something more specific: a stay that's long, but doesn't feel like a compromise.

04 · What they do
City Apartments

Three answers to the same objection.

In-house management, flexible corporate plans, apartments designed for longer stays — the three things a corporate travel buyer actually needs reassurance on, each earning one card, not one paragraph.

05 · The scale, and a signature
City Apartments

Numbers, then a name.

Six hard numbers first — scale, tenure, accreditation — then a short note signed by the CEO, not a marketing team. The scale argument and the personal one sit side by side instead of competing.

06 · The group
City Apartments

The parent company, treated as an upgrade.

Group ownership is framed as an expanded network — preferred rates and experiences across Europe — rather than a footnote, with its own photo grid instead of a single logo lockup.

07 · The newsroom
City Apartments

Proof the site is alive.

A lightweight editorial section — accreditation news, sustainability, a corporate-stays explainer, a company announcement — signals a company still publishing, not a homepage frozen at launch.

08 · The close
City Apartments

The close is a person, not a form.

Real faces instead of a generic contact icon, next to a single link — "Tell us what you need" — phrased like a conversation starter, not a lead-capture CTA.

09 · The footer
City Apartments

Even the footer stays on-brand.

Dark, quiet, and functional — sitemap, contact, social, and a newsletter signup — closing the page without introducing a single new idea.
